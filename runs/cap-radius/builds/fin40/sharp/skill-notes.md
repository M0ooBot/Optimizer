# Skill notes: close-rounded-wingtip on fin40 / sharp

These notes are for the maintainer of `.agents/skills/close-rounded-wingtip/SKILL.md`. As the ground rules required, I read only the skill, my parent folder and my own output folder. I did not open the linked Wing B study.

## What I did, step by step

**Build 1: attachment curves.** I took both cut edges from the parent: the last pole row (Z = 240) of each skin in `parent.step`, refined exactly by knot insertion. The cap shares those poles, so both joins are the parent's own edges. Sewing at 1e-6 mm left no free edges, and the separation is 2.3e-11 mm.

**Build 2: 3D perimeter.** The outline is locked and given (`parent.tip_outline`). It already lies in Y = 0, it is tangent to the parent LE and TE, and it even matches their curvature, because its h^3 term starts at third order. The only work was to rewrite it in a parameter w with h = 1 - w^2, so that it is regular through the crown, where dX/dh is infinite. Because the elevation check against rise and twist is trivial for a fin with no rise or twist, I skipped it.

**Build 3: wrap guides.** I did not draw a handful of guides. I defined a continuous family: one planar guide per parent chord station u. Each one runs on a straight side-view line from the cut point A(u) to a perimeter point L(u), with a half-thickness law along it. Fixing that law was the real design decision, and step 4 below explains it. The finite lip radius comes from the law itself, as the end radius of an ellipse.

**Build 4: boundary surface.** There is no boundary-surface feature in CadQuery or OCP. I implemented one as a single B-spline face whose profile direction runs lower cut edge -> perimeter -> upper cut edge (v = -1, 0, +1) and whose guide direction is u. The boundary rows are fixed to the parent's cut-edge poles and the interior poles are a least-squares fit to the guide family (maximum error 1.5e-6 mm). The thickness law I chose, Y^2 = 0.0144 [a^2 (1 - h^3) - (X - m)^2], is the parent's own law (Y^2 = 0.0144 (X - X_le)(X_te - X)) with the chord ends moved onto the outline. The two differ by a^2 h^3, so tangency and curvature match at the attachment by construction: the measured tangent-plane angle is 2.5e-5 deg and the curvature tensor differs by at most 1.3e-3 /mm. One face spans both skins, so there is no perimeter seam and the "common tangent plane" clause does not apply.

**Build 5: printable radius.** I treated the radius rule as a design input. I predicted the lip radius of the law analytically from the curvature of the implicit surface before building anything: at least 0.5765 mm. The rebuilt STEP reads the same, 0.5765 mm, on the lip at the TE side near Z = 260.

**Build 6: progressive turn.** For this law the turn tightens steadily into the lip. No trace shows a tight bend that flattens again before the lip, and none of the 77 traces reverses its bending.

**Verify 1 to 5.** I did all five, every one on the reimported STEP. I wrote a NumPy evaluator for the STEP B-splines and cross-checked it against OCC GeomLProp, then ran the joins, 77 plane sections, a dense principal-curvature map with refined local maxima, a numerical zebra across both joins, and CAD-normal renders.

## Design iterations: 3 builds, one shape

The surface shape never changed. All three builds fit the same analytic law, and every build met the surface radius rule (smallest principal radius 0.5765 mm). Only the guide family changed, that is, which perimeter point L(u) each chord station is sent to.

1. **w(u) = 0.2 (2u - 1) - 0.8 cos(pi u).** Here the perimeter speed is non-zero at the corners. All surface checks passed and the B-spline was well conditioned. But near the corners the guide planes cut the lip obliquely, because the side-view guide line almost runs along the outline. A guide's own curvature in its plane then fell below 0.4 mm for u < 0.25 and u > 0.75: 0.019 mm at u = 0.005 and 0.365 mm at u = 0.2. Read literally, that breaks build step 3 ("give the turn through the perimeter a finite lip radius that meets the printable minimum"), even though the surface itself is fine.
2. **w(u) = -cos(pi (u - 0.1 sin 2 pi u)).** Here the perimeter point moves as u^2 near the corners, as the cut point does, so every guide plane stays within 1.15x of the lip's normal plane. The guides then met 0.4 mm everywhere. But the parametrization now collapses parabolically at the corners, and the B-spline went bad within about 0.02 mm of the TE point. The measurements there showed a false 0.0008 mm radius, k2 = -81 /mm, and a 5.4 /mm curvature jump across the join 0.016 mm from the TE point.
3. **Final.** I kept build 2's mapping and added a 2 % linear share (EPS = 0.02), which makes the map polar within the last few percent of chord. The guide-plane radius is at least 0.508 mm in the thick zone and at least 1.36 x t/2 in the thin zone, and every surface and join check passes.

## Where the skill was unclear, ambiguous or wrong

1. **Which radius the rule measures.** The skill says "every radius along the wrap" and the radius of the guide's turn. It also asks for section traces ("cuts that follow the sweep") and their signed curvature. By Meusnier's theorem, the radius of a plane section is the surface's normal radius times the cosine of the plane's obliquity. Obliquely cut planes therefore report radii far below the surface radius. On this cap, constant-X planes give section radii down to 0.036 mm and horizontal planes down to 0.10 mm, while the smallest principal radius is 0.577 mm. A guide drawn in a plane that crosses the lip obliquely has the same problem (iteration 1). The skill should say that the printable radius is the principal or normal radius of the surface. It should also say that section and guide-plane traces need to be roughly normal to the lip, or be corrected with the normal-curvature formula. I report both the section radius and the normal radius for every trace.
2. **The "sharp" trailing edge is round here.** The skill assumes a sharp TE ends in a point, so the thin-zone half-round "shrinks to the parent's trailing-edge point". This parent's sections are true ellipses. The trailing edge closes to zero thickness, but as a round nose of radius 0.0072 c = 0.612 mm at the attachment, and the LE is the same (0.634 mm normal to the swept LE). The spec's thickness table falls below 0.8 mm for chord fractions below 0.0015 and above 0.9985, so by the rule's wording both ends sit in the thin zone, where the lip should be "a half-round whose radius is half the local thickness". That is impossible here without putting a crease into the parent's smooth nose. I continued the parent nose instead, so the thin-zone lip is 0.61 to 0.63 mm, rounder than t/2, which I count as meeting the intent ("as round as the section allows"). The rule should read "at least half the local thickness". It should also say what to do when the parent's ends are already round.
3. **"Chord position" of a cap point.** The rule is judged "at the same chord position", but the skill only defines that through the guides. Whether a given cap point is judged against 0.4 mm or against t/2 therefore depends on the guide mapping, which a builder chooses, and a generous mapping could stretch the thin zone over a large part of the cap. My result does not depend on this, because every point meets 0.4 mm except collapsed-end points within 0.003 mm, which are still at least 0.61 mm. The skill should still fix the mapping, for example the nearest attachment chord fraction or the chord fraction of the normal plane.
4. **Corners where the perimeter meets the cut edge.** The guides collapse to points at the LE and TE ends of the cut, and the skill says nothing about it. Iterations 1 and 2 show the trade-off: guides that hit the lip squarely force a degenerate parametrization, and a regular parametrization forces oblique guides. A sentence on keeping guide planes close to the lip's normal plane, without letting the guide spacing collapse quadratically at the corners, would have saved a build.
5. **Guide family vs a few guides.** Steps 3 and 4 assume a few sketched guides and a CAD boundary-surface feature. Without such a feature, a continuous guide family fitted by least squares is the natural equivalent. It also made "sections at and between guides" a matter of choosing stations (I used 15 guide stations and 12 between them).
6. **Zebra definition and tolerances.** The skill asks for "the same reflection pattern as the viewer" and says to judge against "the design's tolerances". I found no viewer definition in `src/` or `tools/` and no tolerance in the spec. I used phase = asin(r . d) / 5 deg, with r the view direction reflected in the CAD normal and d swept through 8 angles in 10 views. I report raw maxima with no pass or fail. The skill should name the reflection model and give default tolerances.
7. **Step 1 of the build** ("check its elevation against the parent surface's rise and twist") and **verify step 5** ("mirrored wing... centered camera") do not apply to a single fin. I skipped them and say so here.
8. **Renders.** My first renders (point splats, then triangle Gouraud) showed dithering and stair-steps on the stripe edges. Following verify step 5, I rechecked with CAD normals: the STEP normals are within 7.1e-5 deg of the design law and the phase along those lines is smooth. The marks are renderer artifacts. It would help if the skill said which kind of renderer it expects.
